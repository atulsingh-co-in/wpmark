# What you can ask

WPMark gives your AI app a few read-only tools for your site. You never
have to name them: ask in plain language and the AI picks what it needs.
Mentioning "WPMark" or "our website" helps it know where to look.

## Understand the site

- "Give me an overview of our website: what we write about, how much
  content we have, and which forms collect leads."
- "Which topics do we cover most? Which do we barely mention?"
- "Find our pages about pricing and summarise what they promise."
- "Read our homepage and tell me whether it's clear who we're for."

## Review incoming leads

- "Summarise this week's enquiries. What are people asking for?"
- "Which leads from the last 30 days look most urgent or most valuable? Why?"
- "Group this month's enquiries by theme and count each one."
- "Draft a short reply I could send to the three most recent enquiries."
  (The AI drafts in the chat; WPMark sends nothing.)

What the AI sees depends on your role: leads with full, masked or no contact
details, or no leads at all. See [Access & privacy](getting-started.md#4-choose-who-sees-what).

## Measure landing pages and campaigns

- "Which landing pages brought the most leads this month?"
- "Compare leads by UTM campaign for the last 8 weeks."
- "Which form gets the most submissions, and from which pages?"
- "Did last month's campaign bring more enquiries than the month before?"

WPMark counts leads by form, page, campaign and week. It doesn't read
traffic numbers (visits, bounce rate) from analytics tools.

## Find content gaps

- "What do customers keep asking in our forms that we have no page about?"
- "Turn the last 50 enquiries into a content plan for next month."
- "Which questions from leads could be answered in an FAQ?"

## Audit SEO

- "List pages with no meta description or focus keyword, most important first."
- "Which pages are set to noindex? Is that intentional?"
- "Find pages that no other page links to."
- "Which pages have duplicate titles?"

Meta descriptions, focus keywords and noindex come from Rank Math. Without
it, WPMark still finds thin, stale, orphan and duplicate-title pages.

## Refresh old content

- "Which posts are over a year old and under 600 words?"
- "Pick the five old posts most worth updating, and say what to add to each."
- "Read our post about [topic] and suggest what's out of date."

## Ready-made playbooks

In Claude, click **+** in the chat box to find WPMark's playbooks:

- **Weekly lead review:** what came in, what matters, what to follow up.
- **Content plan from customer questions:** real questions turned into post ideas.
- **SEO health check:** the gaps worth fixing first.

## Tips for better answers

- **Be specific about time.** "Last 7 days" or "since 1 March" beats "recently".
- **Ask for a format.** "As a table", "top 5 only", "one line each".
- **Follow up.** "Now group those by service", "Which of these has no page?"
- **Check before acting.** The AI reads your real data but can still get
  things wrong. Open the page or lead yourself before making a change.

## What it won't do

WPMark can't publish, edit, trash or delete anything, and it can't send
emails or reply to leads. If you ask, the AI tells you what to do, and you do
it in WordPress. It also can't see things outside your site, such as your
Google Analytics, CRM or inbox.
